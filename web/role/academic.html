📖 正文 · CEFR C1

Brussels levies a 430 million euro fine as it presses the network to halt transatlantic data flows

Brussels has levied a 430 million euro fine on a prominent social network, concluding that its practice of routing European users' personal data to United States servers fell short of the safeguards that EU data protection law requires. Transfers of that kind are lawful only where the destination jurisdiction delivers a level of protection essentially equivalent to the Union's own, and the regulator concluded that the company's contractual assurances were insufficient to bridge that gap. With some thirty million European account holders, the decision signals how far the bloc is prepared to push its digital rules beyond its borders. The company has been ordered to suspend the flows within six months; it contests the findings and has signalled an appeal, leaving both the amount and the timetable open to revision.

🔧 长难句拆解

1. Under EU law, such transfers need strong legal protection, and officials found that protection was missing.

  • 语法点：并列句 + that 引导的宾语从句
  • 讲解：and 前后各是一个完整分句，构成并列句。后一分句中 that protection was missing 作 found 的宾语从句，that 在新闻体中常被省略；从句内部必须用陈述语序（主语 protection + 谓语 was missing），不能写成疑问句语序。

2. The network serves about thirty million users in Europe, so the case affects many people.

  • 语法点：so 引导的结果状语从句
  • 讲解：so 连接因果关系，前因后果，前面的逗号不可省略。serves 用一般现在时表示平台的常态规模；英文新闻常用一般现在时叙述刚发生的事，让读者感到事件仍在发酵。

3. A company spokesperson said it disagrees with the ruling and will appeal, so the final penalty may still change.

  • 语法点：并列谓语 + 情态动词表推测
  • 讲解：disagrees 与 will appeal 由 and 连接并共用主语 it，第二个动词前不再重复主语。may still change 中 may 表示可能性推测，still 提醒结果尚未确定；这类缓和语气在报道未决诉讼时几乎成了固定写法。

✍️ 写作练习

题目：Some people believe that governments should fine technology companies for mishandling personal data, while others think such penalties damage innovation. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Personal data is now one of the most valuable commodities of the internet age, and Europe's fine on a social network asks who should pay when it is mishandled. In my view, financial penalties are necessary, provided they are fair and clearly defined. Supporters of heavy fines argue that money is the only language global companies understand. A technology firm rarely changes its behaviour after a polite warning, but a penalty worth several months of profit concentrates the minds of executives immediately. A fine is therefore not a punishment for success but the price of breaking a promise made to users. Critics counter that such penalties hurt consumers, because companies recover the cost through slower innovation or paid features. That argument is weaker than it looks. Data protection is not a luxury service; it is the condition under which people agree to use a platform at all …

  • B. Pay the fine in two instalments.
