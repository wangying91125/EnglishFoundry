💼 How do you say “430 million euros” in English?

What happened

Regulators in the European Union have fined a large social network 430 million euros. The company sent personal data of European users to servers in the United States. Under EU law, such transfers need strong legal protection, and officials found that protection was missing. The network serves about thirty million users in Europe, so the case affects many people. Officials ordered the company to stop the transfers within six months. A company spokesperson said it disagrees with the ruling and will appeal, so the final penalty may still change.

Vocabulary to know
• regulator (n) — An official body or person that makes sure companies obey the rules of an industry. In context: The Irish regulator opened a new inquiry into how the app handles children's data.
• fine (v) — To make a person or company pay money as a punishment for breaking a rule. In context: Authorities fined the operator after discovering that safety checks had been skipped.
• transfer (n) — The movement of something from one place, system or country to another. In context: Every international money transfer is recorded and checked for fraud.
• personal data (n. phr) — Information about a person that can identify them, such as a name, address or phone number. In context: Giving your date of birth means handing over personal data to a stranger.
• safeguard (n) — A measure designed to prevent harm or misuse. In context: Encryption is a basic safeguard for any company that stores customer records.
• violate (v) — To break a law, rule or agreement. In context: The court found that the site had violated users' privacy rights.
• ruling (n) — An official decision made by a court or a body with authority. In context: The ruling surprised many analysts, who had expected a much smaller penalty.
• appeal (v) — To ask a higher court or authority to examine and change a decision. In context: The firm said it will appeal the decision within the next three months.
